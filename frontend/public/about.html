About

A crowdsourced map of the DriveTest routes people actually drive — built from real evidence, not schematic guesses.

The problem

If you're studying for a G or G2 road test in Ontario, there's no official source telling you which streets your local DriveTest centre actually uses. What exists instead: template-clone sites that show a disclaimed "sample route" for free and gate the real one behind a purchase, or hand-drawn maps explicitly labeled "schematic, not to scale," assembled from anecdotes. Neither is built from anything you could call evidence.

What this is instead

OntarioDriveTestMap reconstructs routes from three real sources:

  • GPS traces — actual recorded drives along real DriveTest routes.
  • Hand-transcribed test-drive videos — turn-by-turn instructions pulled from real footage.
  • Community submissions — routes reported by people who've recently taken or driven a test, validated against real street data and corroborated against each other before being trusted.

These sources are combined into a weighted graph of road segments, where a segment's weight reflects how many independent sources actually agree it belongs to a given route. Routes are reconstructed by walking that graph — not by anyone manually deciding what looks right.

Confidence is data, not decoration

Every route, and every section within a route, is labeled by how well-supported it actually is:

Confirmed Predicted Insufficient evidence

This distinction is never blurred for the sake of a cleaner-looking map. A driving-test candidate could physically execute a turn no one ever confirmed — so predicted or unverified sections are always drawn and labeled differently from confirmed ones, everywhere they appear.

Who this is for

Mostly first-time or anxious test-takers looking for the real streets and turns their local centre uses — often on a phone, often the night before a test, sometimes reviewed in the car itself. There's also a smaller community of contributors who've recently taken or driven a route, and centre-specific discussion where people swap tips and ask questions.

OntarioDriveTestMap is an independent, community-built project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by, or operated on behalf of DriveTest, Serco Canada Inc., or the Ontario Ministry of Transportation.

Design philosophy

This is built as a civic utility, not a product being sold — calm and legible for someone checking a route under real pressure, not slick for a screenshot. Low visual noise, real texture (actual street names, actual vote and source counts) over synthetic polish, and no urgency-manufacturing patterns.
